Lecture 15 · OverviewScope and Must Know

Lecturer: Dr Joseph Ryan. Main source: Atkinson, Anatomy for Dental Students (2013).

Scope: examinable: pp. 1–16. Pages 1–7 are the title, outline and learning outcomes. Pages 8–16 cover pharyngeal pouches, clefts and arches. Face, palate, jaws, tongue and thyroid (p. 17 onwards) are not examinable.

Must know

  • Pouch = endoderm (inside). Cleft = ectoderm (outside).
  • Arch core = ectomesenchyme from the neural crest, plus mesoderm.
  • Arch nerves: 1st V · 2nd VII · 3rd IX · 4th–6th X.
  • A muscle is always supplied by the nerve of its own arch.
  • 3rd pouch → inferior parathyroid + thymus. 4th pouch → superior parathyroid + C cells.
  • Meckel's cartilage (1st arch) → malleus + sphenomandibular ligament.

Key to markers

  • (textbook) — fact from standard embryology texts, not on the slides.

Lecture 15 · Part 1 · p. 8Pharyngeal Apparatus: Basic Layout

PartMade ofPosition
Pharyngeal archMesenchyme core (ectomesenchyme + mesoderm)Bars in the wall of the foregut
Pharyngeal pouchEndodermInner (foregut) side, between arches
Pharyngeal cleft (branchial groove)EctodermOuter surface, between arches
  • Pouches form from week 4 onwards.
  • Pouches separate adjacent arches and mark the front and back limits of each arch.
  • Number of arches:
    • Early vertebrates: 6 pairs.
    • Humans: the 5th arch is transitory, and the 4th and 6th coalesce into a single arch.
  • Pouches: 4 pairs.

Lecture 15 · Part 2 · p. 9Neural Crest

  • During neural tube closure, cells migrate from the neural crest into the arches.
  • These cells form the ectomesenchyme of each arch.
  • Paraxial mesoderm migrates into the arches by a separate route.
  • Later (Fig. 21.1):
    • Ectomesenchyme → skeletal components and subcutaneous tissue
    • Mesoderm → muscle

Lecture 15 · Part 3 · p. 11Pouch and Cleft Derivatives

StructureDerivative
1st pouchMiddle ear (tympanic) cavity and auditory tube
1st cleftExternal acoustic meatus
1st membrane (where 1st pouch meets 1st cleft)Tympanic membrane (eardrum)
2nd pouchPalatine tonsil
3rd pouchInferior parathyroid gland and thymus
4th pouchSuperior parathyroid gland, and the ultimobranchial body, which forms the parafollicular (C) cells of the thyroid
Exam trap: the 3rd pouch gives the inferior parathyroid and the 4th pouch gives the superior parathyroid. The 3rd-pouch tissue migrates further down with the thymus, so it ends up lower. (textbook)
  • The main thyroid gland (follicular cells) does not come from a pouch. It descends from the foramen caecum of the tongue (shown in the p. 11 figure).
Check: the slide says the 1st pouch and cleft form the "outer and middle ears." More precisely, the cleft forms only the external acoustic meatus. The auricle forms from swellings on the 1st and 2nd arches. (textbook)

Lecture 15 · Part 4 · p. 13Structure of Every Arch

ComponentBecomes
Cranial nerveSupplies everything derived from that arch
Ectomesenchyme (from neural crest)Skeletal elements, connective tissue coverings, muscle attachments
Paraxial mesodermSkeletal muscle
Lateral mesodermAortic arch artery
  • Each arch is covered by ectoderm on the outside and in the clefts.
  • Each arch is lined by endoderm on the inside and in the pouches.

Lecture 15 · Part 5 · pp. 14–16Arch Derivatives

ArchSkeletalNerveMuscles
1st (mandibular) Meckel's cartilage → malleus, sphenomandibular ligament; pterygoquadrate bar → incus CN V (V3) Muscles of mastication, anterior belly of digastric, mylohyoid, tensor veli palatini, tensor tympani
2nd (hyoid) Stapes, styloid process, stylohyoid ligament, lesser horns + upper body of hyoid CN VII Muscles of facial expression, posterior belly of digastric, stylohyoid, stapedius
3rd Greater horns + lower body of hyoid CN IX Stylopharyngeus
4th–6th Laryngeal cartilages: thyroid from the 4th; cricoid and arytenoid from the 6th (Fig. 21.5) CN X Muscles of the soft palate, pharyngeal constrictors, muscles of the larynx
Rule: a muscle is always supplied by the cranial nerve of its own arch.

Pairs that commonly appear in exams

  • Tensor veli palatini is a soft palate muscle but comes from the 1st arch, so it is supplied by V3. All other soft palate muscles are supplied by X.
  • Tensor tympani (1st arch, V3) and stapedius (2nd arch, VII).
  • Anterior belly of digastric (1st arch, V3) and posterior belly (2nd arch, VII).
  • Stylopharyngeus is the only muscle of the 3rd arch and the only muscle supplied by IX.
  • 4th arch nerve = superior laryngeal branch of X, which supplies cricothyroid. 6th arch nerve = recurrent laryngeal branch of X, which supplies the other intrinsic laryngeal muscles. (textbook)
Check:
  • Incus: the slides (Atkinson) derive the incus from the pterygoquadrate bar. Most embryology texts (Langman, Moore) derive both malleus and incus from Meckel's cartilage. Either way, the incus is 1st arch.
  • Hyoid: the slides list "hyoid" under both the 2nd and 3rd arches. The split is as shown in the table above.
Link to Lecture 14: the sphenomandibular ligament is the Meckel's cartilage remnant that shields the mylohyoid nerve during an IAN block. See Sphenomandibular ligament.
